# Code of conduct

Fledgetone is made by people who want making music to be open to everyone. This code of conduct
covers everyone taking part in the project: in issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, problem
reports and anywhere else you speak for or about the project.

## What we expect

- Be kind and patient. People here range from first-time musicians to experienced developers, and
  most questions are new to someone.
- Assume good intent, and ask before assuming a mistake was deliberate.
- Give feedback on the work, not the person. Say what you'd change and why.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change, and that "no" or "not now" isn't personal.
- Respect people's time, privacy and boundaries.

## What isn't acceptable

- Harassment, threats, insults or personal attacks, in public or in private messages.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments about anyone's identity, background, body, ability, age,
  gender, sexuality, religion, nationality or level of experience.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Repeatedly derailing discussions, or continuing after being asked to stop.
- Sharing music, samples or code you don't have the right to share.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour worries you, email martin@twobirdsworkshop.com. Say what happened, where,
and who was involved, with links if you have them. Reports are read by the maintainer and kept
private; we won't share who reported something without your permission. If the report is about
the maintainer, say so and it will be handled with that in mind.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides what to do, which can be a private word, a public note, removing comments,
code or other contributions, or a temporary or permanent ban from the project. We'll tell the
person reporting what we did, where we can. Anyone asked to stop a behaviour is expected to stop
straight away.

This code applies to the project's spaces. It also applies elsewhere when someone is representing
the project, or when behaviour elsewhere makes the project's spaces unsafe for others.
