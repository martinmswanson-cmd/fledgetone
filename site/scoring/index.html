ScoringWrite to the picture

Hit points

Every cut on the beat

Load an MP4 or WebM and it runs along the timeline with a preview window that follows the playhead. Find the cuts marks every hard cut with a hit point locked to its time.

FIT › Hit points then finds the tempos that land the music on those hits, from a bar down to a beat, and shows how close each one gets. For a cut no tempo can catch, add a tempo change or a time signature change right there.

  • Hit points stay on their frame when the tempo changes
  • Tempo changes, glides and time signatures
  • The tempo finder and tap tempo, from the picture

Timecode

Timecode you can trust

Work in your project's frame rate: 23.976, 24, 25, 29.97, 29.97 drop-frame, 30 or 60 fps. Type a timecode to go there, switch the ruler to time, and snap clips, cuts and markers to frames.

The Video tab keeps it all together: the film, its sound, where it starts, the cuts, dialogue dips, the tempo finder, cut to length and the exports.

  • Drop-frame counted the way your editor counts it
  • Snap to frames, not just beats
  • The video is saved with the project

Hand-off

Back to your editor

Export markers for video editors sends your cues to Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut Pro, starting at 01:00:00:00 if your timeline does. Export the mix, stems for every track at 48 kHz, or the music straight onto your MP4.

  • Markers with their timecodes
  • Stems that add up to the mix
  • The music on your video, its own sound kept if you like

ToolsFor dialogue, deadlines and every cut-down

Dip under dialogue

It listens to the film's own sound for voices and dips the music for every line, bridging the pauses between words. Each dip is ordinary volume automation you can move.

One score, every length

Cut to length makes 6, 15, 30, 60 and 90-second versions for trailers and spots, every edit on a bar, ending on the score's own ending.

An underscore version

Export the cue with a version that leaves out the lead melody, and one without drums, for scenes with voice-over.

A recorded orchestra

Strings, brass, woodwinds, harp, timpani and a twenty-voice choir, recorded at several loudnesses, with swells, vibrato and legato you can draw.

Real rooms

Put the whole score in a recorded hall, or give each section its place on the stage, so the orchestra sounds like it was in one room.

Sound design

Hits, risers, whooshes and ambience from the sound effects maker, on tracks next to the music, placed to the frame.
