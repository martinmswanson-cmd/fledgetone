For game developers

Music that plays along with your game.

Write a soundtrack that changes with the player: sections that become game states, layers that build with the action, stingers for the big moments and the sound effects, then drop it all into Godot, Unity, a web game, FMOD or Wwise.

Fledgetone makes the music and the sounds, not the game: you build your game in your own engine and drop them in.

Adaptive musicMusic that follows the player

States

Sections become states

Put markers between the parts of your song and each one becomes a state the game can switch to: Explore, Combat, Boss. The Game tab rehearses it: click a state and the music moves there on the next bar, beat or phrase, and loops it.

The transition matrix sets how every state hands over to every other: a cut, a drum fill, a riser, an impact, a crossfade, one of your stingers, or a bridge of a bar or two written from your song to lead into the next chord.

  • Switch on the next bar, beat, phrase or at once
  • Fills, risers and bridges on your own instruments
  • Shuffled pieces and silences, so a long loop never wears out

Intensity

Layers that build with the action

Put your tracks in stem groups (ambient, tension, percussion, climax) and draw how loud each plays as the intensity rises. One number from the game, from 0 to 100%, brings the drums in for a fight and the strings in for a discovery.

A parameter moves several things at once from one value the game sets, such as danger or health, and effects like underwater, muffled and low health colour the whole mix.

  • A curve per stem group
  • Parameters and effects the game controls
  • Costumes: the same music played another way

Stingers

Stingers for every moment

Short cues in the song's key and tempo for an item found, a level up, a checkpoint, a victory or a game over, played on the beat over whatever is playing.

Audition like the game renders every state exactly as the game kit exports it and plays it with the kit's own player, so what you hear in the studio is what ships.

  • In the song’s key and tempo, on the beat
  • Audition with the real player, effects and all
  • Every stinger exported as its own file

Sound effectsThe sounds games are full of

Sound FX

Make a sound, then a hundred like it

The sound effects maker makes menus, hits, pickups, magic, footsteps, jingles and ambience loops: pick a kind (Coin, Sword hit, Level up, Teleport, Rain, Forest...) and every click makes a new one. Many are built on real recordings of the thing itself, with a modern engine for clean, stereo sound.

Add to game keeps a sound with your song, and the game kit carries it with its variations, so footsteps and sword hits never sound like the same sample twice.

  • Over 100 kinds in 12 groups, from UI clicks to thunder
  • Recorded layers (public domain) for real footsteps, hits and nature
  • Variations, seamless loops and even loudness across a set

ExportStraight into your engine

Export game kit makes one .zip with everything the game needs: every state as a seamless loop, the stems, the transitions and bridges, the stingers, your sound effects, a manifest, and a player that follows your rules.

  • Godot 4

    Unzip it into your project: a player, your song and an example scene. Press F6 and play the states, intensity and stingers from the keyboard.

  • Unity

    Unzip it into your project: everything lands in Assets/Fledgetone, with an example component that gives you a button for every state and sound.

  • Web games

    A JavaScript player for the browser, or the web runtime kit: the song plays live from its project, with no audio files to download.

  • FMOD and Wwise

    A package with your loops and stingers and scripts that set up the events, ready to import.

  • Plain files

    An intro and a seamless loop with loop points, or every track as a layer, all the same length. They work in any engine.

# In Godot, with AdaptiveMusic.gd as an autoload called Music
Music.load_song("res://bos_music/my_song/")
Music.play("Explore")
Music.set_state("Combat")     # switches the way your transition rules say
Music.set_intensity(0.8)      # bring the layers in
Music.stinger("Victory")
Music.play_sfx("Coin")

Music you make is yours to use in your game. A few recorded drum kits come from a collection with no stated licence, so for a commercial release the credits say which to swap.
