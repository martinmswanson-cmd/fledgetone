Open Fledgetone on a phone and you get **Pocket**: the same instruments, effects and songs as the studio, on one screen made for a thumb. In this lesson you'll make a short house song on it: a beat you tap in with a finger, off-beat chords, a bass line, a vocal if you like, then a build-up and an MP3 to send.

Be clear on one thing first: **Pocket is in its early stages, and the studio on a computer is much better.** Some things are missing on the phone and some may not work as they should yet. Pocket is for sketching an idea on the bus; the studio is where you finish it. Every song you start on the phone opens on a computer exactly as it is, so nothing is lost by starting small. The guide lists what Pocket has and what it leaves out: [Fledgetone Pocket: on a phone](/guide/fledgetone-pocket-on-a-phone/).

### The screen

Open fledgetone.com on your phone. A notice says Pocket is an early version; tap **Continue** (tick **Don't show this again** if you've read it). Everything is on one screen:

- **At the top**, the song: its name, tempo and where it's playing. Tap it for the song's settings; **Share** is on the right.
- **The grid** is the whole song: a row for each **section** (A, B, C…) and a column for each track. Each cell shows what that track plays in that section.
- **The transport**: undo, **Rec**, **Play** and redo.
- **The drawer** at the bottom is the instrument of the cell you tapped. Its handle makes it bigger or smaller.

### Start a song

1. Pocket opens on **What are we making?** (if it opened your last song, tap the round button at the top left).
2. Tap **Blank song**. You get four tracks with nothing written yet: **Drums**, **Slap Bass**, **Space Clav** and **Brass Stabs**, at 122 BPM, and one section, **A**, four bars long.
3. Tap the song's name at the top (*Untitled*), call it *Pocket House*, and set the **Key** to **A minor**. Close the sheet with the **X**.

{{shot:pocket-new}}

> Would rather start from something finished? Tap any starter under **Blank song**: it plays straight away. The song you had is kept: **Carry on with** brings it back from the same screen.

### Tap in a beat

1. Tap the **Drums** cell in row **A**. The drawer becomes the drums: big pads, laid out like a classic pad sampler, with the kick, snare, clap and hat along the bottom.
2. Tap **Draw** in the drawer. Now it shows one drum at a time, and its 16 steps for one bar, four to a row (a row per beat).
3. With **Kick** picked along the top, tap steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 (the first of each row).
4. Pick **Clap** and tap steps 5 and 13.
5. Pick **Hat** and tap all 16 steps.
6. Tap **Copy to all bars**: bar 1's beat is now in all four bars of A.
7. Tap **Swing** once (15%). The hats now lean back a little instead of ticking like a clock.

Press **Play** (it says **Loop A**: it plays section A over and over). Here's the beat:

```grid
Kick  x...|x...|x...|x...
Clap  ....|x...|....|x...
Hat   xxxx|xxxx|xxxx|xxxx
```

{{shot:pocket-steps}}

The pads play the kit too. Tap **Rec drums** under the grid, and pads you tap while the section plays are written in on the nearest 16th. Tap a wrong step again to take it out.

> Not sure what to tap? **Drummer** writes the whole section's beat in one of 21 styles (try *Four on the floor*), and picking it again gives another take. It's a set of rules, not a recording: change any step after. **Undo** brings your own beat back.

Then tap **Fill** and choose **Snare roll (last beat)**: the end of the section now rolls into what comes next.

### Chords in the key

1. Tap the **Space Clav** cell in row A, then **Draw**. The drawer opens full: this is the piano roll, with a row for each note of A minor (and only those), so whatever you draw fits.
2. Tap **Chords**. Pick the style **Off-beat stabs**, then **i – VI – III – VII (epic)**.

Play it. The clav plays four chords, one a bar, on the off-beats: **A minor, F major, C major, G major**.

{{shot:pocket-chords}}

> Pick the key yourself before you write (as you did when you named the song). Left alone, the key is worked out from the notes already in the song, so after a few notes it can change (C major and A minor have the same notes).

### A bass line

1. Tap **Slap Bass** in the names above the grid. The piano roll stays open, now for the bass.
2. Tap **1/8** three times, to **1 bar**: each note you draw is now a bar long.
3. Tap the **A2** row at the start of bar 1. Scroll sideways with two fingers (or by dragging the numbers along the top) and tap **F2** at the start of bar 2, **C3** at bar 3 and **G2** at bar 4.

{{shot:pocket-bass}}

The bass now follows the chords' roots. Tap a note name to hear it on its own. Tap a note to select it: the bar above makes it shorter or longer, moves it a note up or down, copies it or deletes it. Drag a note to move it, or its right end to stretch it.

> Want a tune too? Tap **Brass Stabs**, tap the note-length button until it reads **1/8**, and tap a few notes anywhere in the roll. They're all in A minor, so nothing clashes.

Tap **Play** in the drawer to leave the piano roll: the drawer goes back to its size, and the grid shows again.

### Sing over it

You need headphones for this: without them, the microphone records the song from the speaker as well.

1. Tap **+ Track** above the grid, then **Vocals**. A **Vocals** track appears, and the drawer is its microphone.
2. Tap **Record** (or **Rec vocal** under the grid), and allow the microphone when the phone asks. Pocket plays section A once, records you over it and stops by itself.
3. Pick the voice's sound: **Clean lead**, **Aggressive lead**, **Ad-lib**, **Phone / radio**, **Dark and wide** or **Backing stack**.

{{shot:pocket-vocals}}

Each take in the **Takes** list has three buttons: **double** (two more performances either side, for a thicker vocal), **tune** (pitch correction to the key, from gentle to robotic) and delete.

### Mix it

1. Tap **Mix all** above the grid. The drawer becomes a mixer: a fader for each track, with **M** (mute) and **S** (solo).
2. Pull the **Space Clav** fader down a little if the chords cover the vocal.
3. The **Master** strip at the end sets the whole song, and **Loud** makes it louder with the peaks shaved.
4. Tap **Done**. Each track's own **Mix** (in its drawer) also has its reverb; its **Sound** swaps the instrument for another of its kind, and the notes stay.

{{shot:pocket-mix}}

### Arrange it

The section letters arrange the song. The one selected has a **⋯** on it: tap it again (or hold any letter) for what you can do from there.

1. Tap **A**'s **⋯**, then **Play it twice**. A copy of A appears after it, and it's selected now.
2. Tap its **⋯** and **Play it twice** again. The song is three A's, 12 bars. The copies are linked: change one and the others change too, until you **Make it its own**.
3. Tap the third section's **⋯**, then **Build-up into it**. A riser and a snare roll build through the bars before it, on two new tracks, and a hit lands on its first beat.

{{shot:pocket-arrange}}

The letters change to **A**, **B**, **C**: sections that play the same thing share a letter, and the build-up made the second and third sections different. Tap **Loop** above the grid so it says **Song**, then **Play**: four bars to settle in, four bars of build-up, and the drop.

### Share it

1. Tap **Share** at the top right, then **Export an MP3**. Pocket plays the song through into an MP3, matched to the loudness streaming sites use, and hands it to the phone's share sheet (or saves it, on a phone without one).
2. **Share a link to the song** sends a link that opens the song in Fledgetone, to hear and change. Recorded vocals aren't in links: use **Save** under **Project file** for those.

{{shot:pocket-share}}

Then open the link on a computer. It opens in the full studio, every track and note where you left it (each section starts at a marker on the timeline), ready for the things Pocket doesn't have: automation, plugins and the full mixer. **Use the full studio here** (in the song's settings) switches this device to the studio too, and **Help › Pocket, the phone version** in the studio switches back.

### What's next

You've made a whole song on a phone: a tapped-in beat, chords and bass in key, a vocal, a mix, a build-up and an MP3. Take it to a computer and carry on in the studio: [Arrange a full song](/learn/arrange-a-song/) shows how to turn these 12 bars into an intro, verses and choruses, and [Make your first beat](/learn/first-beat/) does the beat in more depth.
