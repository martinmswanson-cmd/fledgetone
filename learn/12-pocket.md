level: Beginner

---
Open Fledgetone on a phone and you get **Pocket**: the same instruments, effects and songs as the studio, with a simpler screen made for a thumb. In this lesson you'll make a short house song on it: a beat you paint with a finger, off-beat chords, a bass line, a vocal if you like, then a build-up, a drop and an MP3 to send.

Be clear on one thing first: **Pocket is in its early stages, and the studio on a computer is much better.** Some things are missing on the phone and some may not work as they should yet. Pocket is for sketching an idea on the bus; the studio is where you finish it. Every song you start on the phone opens on a computer exactly as it is, so nothing is lost by starting small. The guide lists what Pocket has and what it leaves out: [Fledgetone Pocket: on a phone](/guide/fledgetone-pocket-on-a-phone/).

### Start a song

Open fledgetone.com on your phone. A notice says Pocket is an early version; tap **Continue** (tick **Don't show this again** if you've read it). Six tabs run along the bottom: **Song**, **Beat**, **Notes**, **Vocals**, **Mix** and **Share**. Play, stop, the loop and undo sit across the top.

1. On the **Song** tab, tap **New**.
2. Tap **Empty song**. You get four tracks with nothing written yet: **Drums**, **Slap Bass**, **Space Clav** and **Brass Stabs**, at 122 BPM, with the loop on bars 1 to 5.
3. Tap the name at the top (*Untitled*) and call it *Pocket House*.

{{shot:pocket-new}}

> Would rather start from something finished? Open a group under **New** and tap any starter. The song you had is kept: a **Restore** card on the Song tab brings it back.

### Paint a beat

1. Tap the **Beat** tab, then **New** under **Pattern**. A one-bar pattern, **Bar 1**, appears.
2. Under the pads, the **Steps** grid has a row per drum and 16 steps for the bar. Tap the **Kick** on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13.
3. Tap the **Clap** on steps 5 and 13.
4. For the **Hat**, press the first step and slide your finger along the row to the end: every step you pass turns on. (Sliding up or down still scrolls the page.)
5. Drag **Swing** to about 20%. The hats now lean back a little instead of ticking like a clock.

Press play at the top. Here's the beat you've painted:

```grid
Kick  x...|x...|x...|x...
Clap  ....|x...|....|x...
Hat   xxxx|xxxx|xxxx|xxxx
```

{{shot:pocket-steps}}

The big pads play the kit too. Turn on **Rec**, press play, and pads you tap while the song is inside the pattern are written in on the nearest 16th. Tap a wrong step again to take it out.

> Not sure what to paint? **Drummer** writes the whole pattern in one of 21 styles (try *Four on the floor*), and picking it again gives another take. It's a set of rules, not a recording: change any step after. **Undo** brings your own pattern back.

### Make it four bars

1. Tap **Repeat**. A copy appears after it as **Bar 2**.
2. Tap **Bar 2**, then **Repeat** again. Do the same once more, so you have four patterns, bars 1 to 4.
3. With **Bar 4** picked, tap **Fill** and choose **Snare roll (last beat)**. The end of bar 4 now rolls into the next bar.

> Always tap the newest pattern before you tap **Repeat** again. If you repeat **Bar 1** twice, the second copy lands on top of the first at bar 2, and you hear it twice as loud.

### Chords in the key

The **Notes** tab is a grid with only the notes of the song's key in it, two octaves at a time, so whatever you tap fits.

1. Tap **Notes**, then the **Space Clav** button at the top, then **Start a part**. A two-bar part appears.
2. Tap the **2 bars** box and choose **4 bars**.
3. Open the key list and pick **A minor**.
4. Tap **Chords**. Pick the style **Off-beat stabs**, then **i – VI – III – VII (epic)**.

Play it. The clav plays four chords, one a bar, on the off-beats: **A minor, F major, C major, G major**.

{{shot:pocket-chords}}

> Pick the key yourself before you write. Left alone, the key is worked out from the notes already in the song, so after a few notes it can change (C major and A minor have the same notes) and the rows shift under your finger.

### A bass line

1. Tap **Slap Bass** at the top, then **Start a part**, and make it **4 bars** as before.
2. Tap **1/4**: each note you add is now a beat long.
3. The numbers **1 2 3 4** above the grid pick the bar. On bar 1, slide along the **A2** row: four A notes, one a beat.
4. On bar 2, slide along **F2**. On bar 3, **C3**. On bar 4, **G2**.

{{shot:pocket-bass}}

The bass now follows the chords' roots. Tap a row's name to hear that note on its own, and tap a note to take it away. The arrows at the top right move the grid an octave.

> Want a tune too? Pick **Brass Stabs**, start a part, choose **1/8** and tap a few notes anywhere on the grid. They're all in A minor, so nothing clashes.

### Sing over it

You need headphones for this: without them, the microphone records the song from the speaker as well.

1. Tap **Vocals**, then **Record**. Allow the microphone when the phone asks.
2. With the loop on, Pocket plays the loop once (bars 1 to 5), records you over it and stops by itself. With the loop off, it records from the playhead until you tap **Stop**.
3. The take lands on a new **Vocals** track, where you sang it. Pick its sound: **Clean lead**, **Aggressive lead**, **Ad-lib**, **Phone / radio**, **Dark and wide** or **Backing stack**.

{{shot:pocket-vocals}}

Each take in the **Takes** list has three buttons: **double** (two more performances either side, for a thicker vocal), **tune** (pitch correction to the key, from gentle to robotic) and delete. **+ Track** adds another vocal track, for harmonies or ad-libs with their own sound.

### Mix it

1. Tap **Mix**. Each track has a card: its **Level**, **M** (mute), **S** (solo) and how much **Reverb** it sends.
2. Pull the **Space Clav** level down a little if the chords cover the vocal.
3. Tap **Sound: Slap Bass** to swap the instrument for another bass: drums swap for drums and pitched instruments for pitched ones, and the notes stay.
4. At the top, the **Master** level sets the whole song, and **Clipper** makes it louder with the peaks shaved.

{{shot:pocket-mix}}

### Arrange it

The **Arrange** tools on the **Song** tab work on the loop.

1. Tap **Song**, then **Repeat the loop** twice. Bars 1 to 4 are copied twice, so the song runs to bar 12, and the loop moves onto the newest copy.
2. In **Tracks**, tap the Space Clav clip at bar 5. It opens on the Notes tab: tap **Loop** there, and the loop is now bars 5 to 9.
3. Back on **Song**, tap **Build-up and drop**. A riser and a snare roll build through bars 5 to 8 on two new tracks, and an impact hits the drop at bar 9.

{{shot:pocket-arrange}}

Turn the loop off (the loop button at the top), tap the square **Stop** to go back to bar 1 and play: four bars to settle in, four bars of build-up, and the drop. **Speed up into the drop** is another one to try on the same loop: the end of it speeds up and snaps back on the drop.

### Share it

1. Tap **Share**, then **Export an MP3**. Pocket plays the song through into an MP3, matched to the loudness streaming sites use, and hands it to the phone's share sheet (or saves it, on a phone without one).
2. **Share a link to the song** sends a link that opens the song in Fledgetone, to hear and change. Recorded vocals aren't in links: use **Save** under **Project file** for those.

{{shot:pocket-share}}

Then open the link on a computer. It opens in the full studio, every track and note where you left it, ready for the things Pocket doesn't have: the piano roll, automation, plugins and the full mixer. **Use the full studio here** switches this device to the studio too, and **Help › Pocket, the phone version** in the studio switches back.

### What's next

You've made a whole song on a phone: a painted beat, chords and bass in key, a vocal, a mix, a build-up and drop, and an MP3. Take it to a computer and carry on in the studio: [Arrange a full song](/learn/arrange-a-song/) shows how to turn these 12 bars into an intro, verses and choruses, and [Make your first beat](/learn/first-beat/) does the beat in more depth.
